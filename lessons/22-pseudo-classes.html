lesson 22 · interactive

Selectors with a mood.

A pseudo-class selects an element by its state, not its name: being hovered, focused, checked, disabled, or being the third child of twelve. Twelve of them cover 95% of real work — this lesson makes each one visible.

01 · :hover · :focus · :active

The interaction trio

Three moments every clickable thing has: cursor over it (:hover), keyboard on it (:focus), finger pressing it (:active). The readout shows which ones match right now — stack them by doing two at once.

hover · press · Tab

state: idle


            

              Order matters in the stylesheet:
              :hover before
              :focus before
              :active — otherwise the press state gets
              overwritten. Old mnemonic: LoVe / HAte.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          
02 · :visited · :checked · :disabled

          
States with memory

          

            :visited remembers where you've been (browsers limit it to
            color, for privacy). :checked powers CSS-only toggles — the
            card below obeys the checkbox with zero JavaScript.
            :disabled greys out what can't be used.

04 · :not() · :is() · :where()

          
The logic operators

          

            :not() excludes. :is() groups with an OR — and keeps
            the specificity of its strongest argument. :where() does the
            same grouping but contributes zero specificity. Same matches,
            different weight.
          

        

        

          

            

              
              
same matches, different weight

            

            

              

              

            

          

          

            

              
              
              
            

            

            

              Use :where() when you want the grouping to be easy to override
              later — it adds nothing to the specificity fight.

nth-child counts

            

              an+b from 1, not 0. odd/even are shortcuts for 2n+1 / 2n. Zebra
              stripes in one selector, forever.
            

            
↳ hover: the odds light up

          

          

            

              

                :is(.a, .b) → weight of the strongest:where(.a, .b) → weight 0-0-0
              

            

            
:is vs :where

            

              Identical matching, different specificity. :is for "these count",
              :where for "group but stay overridable".
