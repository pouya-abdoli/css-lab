lesson 35 · interactive

The strongest selector wins.

When two rules target the same element, CSS doesn't guess — it scores each selector into a tuple (IDs, classes, elements), compares column by column, and the higher one wins. Above the ladder sit inline styles; above everything sits !important.

01 · the five ranks

A ladder, not a lottery

From the top: !important exits the game entirely; then inline styles; then IDs; then classes, attributes and pseudo-classes sharing one column; then elements and pseudo-elements. Click a rank to inspect it.

04 · !important

          
The truce-breaker

          

            !important doesn't add specificity — it
            exits the comparison. A declaration marked important beats
            every normal declaration, even one with a higher score. Toggle it
            and watch a bare p defeat an ID.
          

        

        

          

            

              
              
weak selector, nuclear flag

            

            

              

                styled by the stronger rule
              

              

                
              

              

            

          

          

            

            

              Because it breaks the natural order, it breaks your ability to
              override later — hence "important" should be rare: resets, user
              preferences, a11y overrides. Never layout.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          
05 · ties: source order

          
Equal scores? Later wins

          

            When specificity is identical, CSS falls back to
            source order: the rule that appears
            later in the stylesheet wins (not later in the HTML). Swap
            the order and watch the color flip.
