Size is a negotiation.

You declare a width, the browser decides what actually ships. min and max set guardrails. auto lets content or layout decide. And intrinsic keywords let the content itself set the rules.

01 · width & height

The two rulers every box has

width and height are the most direct sizing tools. Set them and the box obeys — but only within the guardrails of min/max. With box-sizing: border-box, they mean "the box you see", not just the content.

02 · min- & max-

          
The guardrails

          

            min-width and max-width are the floor and ceiling. The
            box wants to be width: 100% of its parent,
            but won't go below the min or above the max. Drag the container to
            see it clamp.

              min-width prevents the box from shrinking below a floor.
              max-width prevents it from growing past a ceiling. The
              dashed guides show where those limits are. When the parent is
              smaller than min, the box clamps up. When larger than max, it
              clamps down.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          
03 · min-height & max-height

          
Same idea, vertical axis

          

            Add more content and the box grows — but
            never past max-height. Remove content and it shrinks — but
            never below min-height. The classic use: a text area that's
            tall enough to read but doesn't explode on long posts.

04 · auto

          
"Let the layout decide"

          

            The default — but what it decides depends on context. In a
            block,
            width: auto
            fills the parent. In an inline-block, it hugs the content. In flex,

              auto is the most honest width: it says "I trust whoever
              renders me". Blocks fill their parent, inline-blocks hug their
              content, flex items share space. Almost always the right choice —
              and almost never written.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          
05 · fit-content · min-content · max-content

          
Let the content set the rules

          

            Three modern keywords that hand sizing over to the text itself.
            Click each — the box snaps to the right answer, and the width
            readout tells you exactly why.

try a length

            

              
              
              
            

            

            

              fit-content hugs text but wraps at the container edge.
              min-content shrinks to the longest word (no breaking).
              max-content goes full width on one line (may overflow).
              Reach for these in flex/grid items where you want children to
              agree on their own widths.
