Accessible is usable for everyone.

Accessibility isn't a feature — it's the baseline that makes everything else reachable. Contrast, focus rings, fluid type, reduced motion, keyboard flow, and never color-alone: seven habits that turn a page from "looks fine" into "works for everyone."

E · VERYONE · C · OUNTS

seven habits, one baseline…

01 · color contrast

4.5:1 is the floor for text

WCAG AA wants 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text. Pick any two colors and the checker scores them live.

score a pair

Big heading
normal body text at 13px


            

              Large text = ≥24px, or ≥18.66px bold. When in doubt, raise the
              contrast — low vision and bright sunlight both demand it.

03 · font sizing & responsive text

          
Fluid type, readable floor

          

            Fixed px stays huge on a phone;
            clamp() scales with the viewport but never
            drops below a readable floor. Drag the viewport and compare.

04 · reduced motion

          
Motion is opt-out-able

          

            Vestibular disorders make animation genuinely uncomfortable. Respect
            prefers-reduced-motion. Toggle it here —
            the dot stops. Your OS setting is detected too.
          

        

        

          

            

              
              
the dot obeys

            

            

              

                

              

              

                
              

              

            

          

          

            

            

              Keep essential feedback (like a submit spinner's result) but drop
              decorative motion: parallax, auto-scroll, big entrances.
            

          

        

      


      
      

        

          
05 · keyboard accessibility

          
Everything reachable, in order

          

            Real <button>s are focusable and
            activate with Enter/Space;
            <div onclick>s are invisible to the

Cheat codes from lesson 41
