px is a fact. The rest are relationships.

CSS speaks nine size units. px stands alone — absolute. But %, em, rem, vw, vh, dvh, svh, lvh all answer "how big?" by pointing at something else: a parent, the root, or the viewport.

05 · dvh · svh · lvh

          
The mobile viewport family

          

            On phones the viewport moves — the URL bar slides away as you
            scroll. svh assumes it's visible (small). lvh assumes
            it's gone (large). dvh follows reality, live. Toggle the bar.

07 · field notes

          
Cheat codes from lesson 04

        

        

          

            

            
absolute vs relative

            

              px is a fixed fact. Everything else — %,
              em, rem, vw, vh — is a relationship: to a parent, the root, or the
              viewport. Pick the relationship you want.
            

            
↳ hover: one stays, one stretches

          

          

            

              rem → type & spacingpx → borders & shadows
            

            
the default split

            

              rem for fonts and layout rhythm (one root change rescales all). px
              where precision matters: 1px borders, 0 2px 8px shadows.
            

            
↳ hover: the split lights up

          

          

            

            
em is local

            

              em scales with the element's own font-size. That's why
              padding: .6em 1.2em keeps a button
              proportional whether its text is 14px or 20px.
            

            
↳ hover: text grows, padding follows

          

          

            

            
the vh family

            

              vh broke mobile fullscreen — URL bars shrink the real viewport.
              svh / lvh / dvh fixed it: small, large, dynamic. Use
              dvh when it's supported.
            

            
↳ hover: the bar eats the viewport
